Case Intake & Routing Framework

Internal framework for safely receiving, reviewing, routing, and following up on technology-safety support requests.

INTERNAL USE: This framework is intended to support consistent, safe, privacy-conscious handling of requests. It does not replace emergency services, qualified legal professionals, cybersecurity professionals, victim advocates, or other specialized human services.
Core Principle:
Take the person's experience seriously without automatically treating every interpretation or explanation as an established fact.
01 — REQUEST RECEIVED

Begin With the Information Provided

Review the submitted request and identify:

  • Date the request was received
  • Contact information provided
  • Type of help requested
  • Description of the person's concern
  • Physical-safety response
  • Device/account-safety response

Do not request passwords, authentication codes, banking information, private keys, or other unnecessary sensitive credentials.

02 — SAFETY FIRST

Check Immediate Risk

Review the two initial safety questions:

  • Is there an immediate concern about physical safety?
  • Is the device or account being used safe right now?

If immediate physical danger is reported, prioritize appropriate emergency or crisis resources and qualified human support.

If the device or account may be monitored or compromised, avoid requesting sensitive information through that potentially unsafe channel.

03 — UNDERSTAND THE REQUEST

Identify What the Person Needs

Possible support categories include:

  • Account or digital-security concerns
  • Online harassment
  • Cyberstalking concerns
  • Impersonation
  • Exposure of personal information
  • Documentation assistance
  • Legal-resource information
  • Emotional or recovery support
  • Multiple forms of support
04 — REVIEW OBSERVABLE INFORMATION

Separate Observation From Interpretation

Review what can actually be documented from the information provided.

Examples of observable information:

  • A message was received from a particular account.
  • An account-login notification appeared.
  • A public post contained specific information.
  • Multiple unwanted contacts occurred at documented times.
  • A particular account or URL was involved.

Avoid presenting an assumed identity, motive, cause, or responsibility as established simply because it appears in a person's interpretation.

05 — PROTECT

Consider Safe Digital-Safety Steps

Depending on the situation, appropriate guidance may include:

  • Reviewing account security
  • Using multi-factor authentication
  • Reviewing active account sessions
  • Preserving relevant documentation when safe
  • Using a safer device or communication method when appropriate
  • Speaking with a trusted person
  • Connecting with a qualified professional

Safety decisions should be individualized rather than treated as one-size-fits-all instructions.

06 — ROUTE

Determine the Appropriate Human Support

  • Immediate danger: emergency or crisis resources
  • Technology abuse or stalking: appropriate victim advocate
  • Account compromise: qualified cybersecurity professional
  • Legal questions: qualified legal resource
  • Image-based abuse: specialized support organization
  • Emotional or recovery needs: appropriate support service
  • Multiple needs: coordinated referrals

TraceGuide's role is to help people find the appropriate next step, not to pretend to be every professional service.

07 — RESPOND

Provide a Safe and Clear Response

A response should generally:

  • Acknowledge the person's request
  • Reflect the information they actually provided
  • Avoid unsupported conclusions
  • Explain the appropriate next step
  • Provide relevant human resources or referrals
  • Respect the person's choices
08 — FOLLOW UP

Close the Loop Safely

When appropriate and safe, determine:

  • Whether the person received the referral
  • Whether their safety situation changed
  • Whether additional support is needed
  • Whether another referral may be appropriate

The person remains in control of their own decisions.

09 — LEARN

Improve TraceGuide Responsibly

With appropriate consent and privacy protections, recurring patterns in support requests can help TraceGuide improve educational resources, referral pathways, safety guidance, and future system design.

Personal information should never be used for learning or public storytelling without appropriate permission and safeguards.

AI Safety Rule

AI may assist with organizing information and identifying observable patterns in submitted material.

AI must not independently establish someone's identity, intent, guilt, responsibility, or the cause of an unexplained event.

Human judgment and appropriate professional support remain essential.

Privacy Rules

  • Collect only information necessary for the support request.
  • Do not request passwords or authentication codes.
  • Do not expose private information publicly.
  • Use safer communication methods when needed.
  • Respect the person's consent and choices.
  • Do not share personal stories without appropriate permission.
  • Do not use the current prototype as a secure evidence vault.
Final Routing Question:
“What is the safest, most appropriate next step for this person based on the information we actually have?”
